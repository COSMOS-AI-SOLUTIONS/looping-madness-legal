2. The short version

  • We don't receive your voice or any other gameplay data. We have no game servers, no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no telemetry and no crash reporting. The game sends us nothing.
  • Your voice stays between you and the players in your party. The game uses your microphone to make music. In multiplayer, your recordings travel from your PC to the other players in your party, through Steam's network (Valve), and are deleted from memory when the round ends.
  • All voice effects happen on your own PC. Pitch, speed, echo, volume, reverse and the other effects are calculated by the game on players' computers. We never process your voice.
  • You decide what is saved, and it is saved on your own PC. Songs and clips you keep are files on your computer, not on any server of ours (we have none). Nothing is published online by the game and there is no public gallery.
  • The microphone is in your control. It never opens before you have answered the in-game recording notice, the game shows ● MIC ON whenever it is open, it only keeps sound while you hold REC, and M mutes it anywhere.

3. What happens on your PC and in your party

We don't collect any of the information in this section: it is handled by the game on players' computers and by Steam. We describe it so you know exactly what the game does.

3.1 Microphone audio (your voice)

  • When the mic is open: only (a) while your Loop-O-Matic screen is open, (b) during your own Stage-time turn, and (c) during the 3-second mic test in Settings. Never in menus, never in the background, never because another player asks for it. It stays closed until you have answered the recording notice, while you have muted it (M), and when you have switched it off in Settings → Privacy.
  • What is kept: while the mic is open, the last few seconds are held in your computer's memory (RAM) and continuously overwritten, so a recording can start exactly on the beat. Only the part you record (while you hold REC, plus a fraction of a second before and after) becomes a recording. When the mic closes, that short memory buffer is wiped.
  • Effects: the game applies a noise gate, levelling and the musical effects you choose (pitch, speed, echo, volume, reverse, stutter, cuts) on your PC. The game does not analyse voices to identify people, does not create voice prints and uses no speech recognition or AI on your voice.

3.2 Recordings in multiplayer

When you play a multiplayer round with "Share my recordings with my party" switched on:

  • each Stage-time take you record goes to the party's host, who sends it to everyone in the party; it lands in every player's clip box for that round and may be used in their songs;
  • at the end of the creation phase your song (which can contain your voice and other players' voices) is sent to everyone in the party and played during the performances. In a normal round, songs are played without the maker's name; names are shown only with the results.
  • the song name you type is shown to everyone in the party (on the club screen, the vote and the results). Other players' song names pass through Steam's text filter before your game shows them.

The audio is compressed (8-bit μ-law, 24 kHz) and kept only in each player's memory until the round ends (or they leave). It passes directly between the players' PCs or through Valve's relay servers. It never reaches us.

If "share" is off, the game doesn't send your voice: you sit out Stage time and build songs from other players' clips and the built-in sounds. If you switch sharing off during a round, your clips are removed from the other players' clip boxes and your voice is left out of later performances from that moment on (see section 6 for limits).

3.3 Songs other players keep

Another player can save (and then export) a song that contains your voice only if you switched on "Let other players keep songs with my voice" (off by default). Taking part in a party never implies this permission. Such a song is saved on that player's own PC.

3.4 Steam

The game runs on Steam (Valve Corporation). To make parties work it uses your Steam ID and display name (shown to the players in your party; streamer mode hides other players' names on your screen), Steam lobbies (lobby name, by default "<your display name>'s party", a 6-character party code and the game version, stored on Valve's servers; public lobbies are listed to other players), friends and rich presence (to show friends' parties and handle invites), lobby chat (through Valve's servers, filtered with Steam's text filter) and Steam Networking, including Valve's relay network, to carry game traffic between players.

Valve handles this under its own terms; see the Steam Privacy Policy. We don't receive this information through the game.

3.5 Your choices in the party

The only consent information the game shares with your party is two yes/no values: whether you share recordings with the party and whether others may keep songs with your voice. Mutes you set are stored only in memory on your own PC and are never sent.

If you use Streamer Mode (section 3.7), your party also sees your stream's platform (Twitch), your channel's name and number, and whether you logged in ("verified") or used Quick connect ("unverified"), so a Streamer Battle can show who is who.

3.6 Files on your own PC

Stored in the game's data folder (Windows: %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Looping Madness Party\) and never uploaded by the game:

  • settings.cfg: your settings, including your recording permissions and, if you used Streamer Mode, its options and the channel name you typed for Quick connect (never a password or login token);
  • saved_clips/: Stage-time takes of your own voice that you chose to keep (★);
  • songs/: song projects you saved; they contain other players' voices only where those players allowed it;
  • exports/: WAV files you exported;
  • logs/: technical log files written by the game engine (Godot), with technical identifiers such as lobby and peer numbers; the engine keeps a few recent log files and replaces older ones.

You can delete any of these at any time (Settings → Privacy, MY SONGS, or the files themselves). Uninstalling through Steam may leave this folder behind: Settings → Privacy → "Your data folder" opens it.

The game doesn't use Steam Cloud: none of these files is copied to Valve's servers or to any other computer. If you play on another PC, your settings, clips and songs stay on the first one (copy them yourself if you want them).

3.7 Streamer Mode (optional, Twitch)

Streamer Mode is only used if you open it and connect. It lets your stream's chat vote in the game.

  • Quick connect (no login): you type your channel name and the game reads your channel's public chat the way any viewer's chat window does. No login, no password, no token.
  • Twitch login (optional, verified): you log in on Twitch's own website (twitch.tv/activate) with a short code. The game asks Twitch only for permission to read chat (user:read:chat). Twitch gives the game a login token, which stays in your PC's memory while the game runs: it is never saved to a file, never shown, never written to logs and never sent to other players or to us. DISCONNECT (or quitting the game) ends it; on DISCONNECT the game also asks Twitch to cancel the token.
  • Chat: the game receives your channel's chat messages from Twitch (and, in a Streamer Battle, the other streamer's channel, read with your own login). It looks only for commands such as !vote A, !rate 4, !tomato or !word banana; a word chosen this way is shown in the game (and so on your stream) and becomes the line you perform. Suggested words first go through Steam's text filter: a word the filter would change is dropped. For each round it keeps, in memory, the viewer's Twitch user number and their vote, to count one vote per viewer. Viewer names and chat histories are not kept; everything is cleared when the round ends. Nothing is sent to Twitch.
  • Streamer Battle: your game sends each vote it reads (viewer number, message number, the vote) to the battle's host, the other streamer's game or yours, through Steam, so both chats can be counted together. Your song travels to the other streamer like any party song (section 3.2).
  • Your stream: your viewers hear and see what your stream shows, including your voice and your songs. That is between you and your streaming platform.
  • OBS link (optional): if you link OBS Studio, the game talks to OBS on your own PC only (127.0.0.1) to mute and unmute one audio source. The OBS password stays in memory for that session.

Twitch handles your Twitch account and chat under its own terms; see the Twitch Privacy Notice.

4. What we receive

  • From the game: nothing. No recordings, songs, Steam IDs, names, chat, logs, statistics or crash reports.
  • From Valve: aggregated sales and store statistics for our Steamworks account, which don't identify players.
  • If you write to us at anisd116@gmail.com: your e-mail address and your message, used only to reply and kept only as long as needed to handle your request. Please don't send us recordings.

We don't sell, rent or share personal data.

5. International transfers

We don't transfer your data anywhere, because we don't receive it. Party traffic, including recordings, goes between the players' own computers or through Valve's relay servers, which can be in other countries. Players in the same party may live in different countries. Steam's part is covered by Valve's privacy policy.

6. Withdrawing a permission, and the limits

You can change every recording permission at any time (Settings → Privacy, or M to mute). Changes apply from that moment on: the mic closes immediately, a take in progress is thrown away, and your party is told your new choice so the game stops using your voice in the round. However:

  • audio that already reached another player's PC during a round stays in their memory until that round ends;
  • a song another player saved or exported while they were allowed to stays on their PC, and the game cannot delete files on other people's computers;
  • other players could always record their own speakers with other software; the game cannot prevent that.

7. Your rights

Because your recordings and settings are on your own PC, you can see, change and delete them yourself at any time (section 3.6). We hold no gameplay data about you, so there is nothing for us to look up, correct or delete. For a question about this policy, or about a message you sent us, contact anisd116@gmail.com.

Depending on where you live you can also contact a data-protection authority: in Tunisia, the INPDP (Instance Nationale de Protection des Données Personnelles); in the EEA/UK (GDPR), your local data-protection authority.

8. Children

Looping Madness Party is for players aged 13 and older. If you are under 13, please don't play it or record your voice in it.

  • If you are 13 or older but under the age of majority where you live, play with the permission of a parent or guardian. Steam's own rules for accounts of minors apply too.
  • The game doesn't ask for or check anyone's age, and we don't receive any gameplay data (section 4), so we hold no information about children.
  • If a child under 13 sends us an e-mail, we delete it and anything in it once we notice, after replying if a reply is needed to protect the child.
  • Parents: Steam Family settings let you limit which games a child can play and who they can talk to.

9. Security

Party traffic uses Steam's encrypted networking. The game checks the size and format of every recording it receives from other players and refuses anything malformed or oversized. Files on your PC are not encrypted by the game; protect your Windows account.

10. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects your recordings, the game shows the recording notice again. The current version is in the game (Legal & Privacy) and at https://cosmos-ai-solutions.github.io/looping-madness-legal/privacy.html.
